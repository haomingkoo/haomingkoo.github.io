Trader Koo

A personal research tool that scans US stocks and crypto each weekday evening, scores trade setups and tests them with simulated trades only.

Open live app Source on GitHub

Python, FastAPI, YOLOv8 and React, deployed on Railway.

The problem

Trading signals that look good in a backtest often fall apart on new data, and a classifier barely better than a coin flip still produces confident buy signals. I wanted every idea to pass checks I would trust before it touched even a simulated book.

What it does

Each weekday at 22:00 UTC a pipeline pulls prices and macro data, detects chart patterns and writes a daily report. Every view flags sources that are stale or missing. Candidate setups go through a rule-based critic with nine checks, and the ones it approves become paper trades sized to risk 0.5% of the simulated account. Trades are paper only: no broker is connected.

How it works

  • Signals from several anglesA technical ensemble weighs trend, mean reversion, momentum, volatility and statistical-arbitrage signals. A pretrained third-party YOLOv8 model detects patterns such as head-and-shoulders and boxes them on the chart.
  • Regime firstA hidden Markov model labels the volatility regime as low, normal or high. A trend signal that runs against the regime is blocked, with no override.
  • A critic that fails closedNine rule-based checks cover conviction, risk to reward, regime, concentration, volatility, caution flags and each setup family’s track record. VIX above 25 blocks new long entries and above 35 blocks all entries. If the regime is unknown and VIX is missing, the critic rejects the trade rather than guessing.
  • Loss breakersA 5% daily loss or a 15% drawdown in the paper book stops new entries.

What was hard

Keeping the research honest. A LightGBM classifier ran in observation mode but never qualified, and it never vetoed, ranked or sized a trade, so I removed it. The repo’s research notes record that the setup score shows no measurable edge, and the app makes no claim that it does.

What stays is the plumbing I would trust: dated sources, a critic that fails closed, and an audit trail of every simulated trade.

Results

The app is live as a research and paper-trading tool, with a daily report, chart analysis and a paper-only audit trail of simulated trades. It is not financial advice, and no real money is at risk.
