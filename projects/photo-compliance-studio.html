Photo ID Studio

For anyone taking their own passport or ID photo: it checks the picture against a country's rules, says what to fix, and returns a crop at the right size.

Open live app Source on GitHub

Python, FastAPI, MediaPipe, OpenCV and rembg, deployed on Railway.

The problem

Passport photo rules are specific and differ by country: the output size, how much of the frame the head fills, how bright and plain the background is, and how recent the photo must be. They are easy to break without noticing, and a rejected photo means another retake and another submission.

What it does

You pick a country profile and upload a photo. The server checks face size, head tilt, eyes, mouth, sharpness, lighting, background, text overlays and photo age, and returns each result with a status and a suggested fix. The page shows each one in plain words.

When the crop can be satisfied it returns a JPEG at the profile's exact pixel size. Profiles cover Singapore, the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and India.

How it works

  • Face landmarks on the serverMediaPipe Face Mesh locates the face and its landmarks. Head roll, yaw and pitch, eye visibility and mouth closure are measured from those points.
  • A crop anchored on the eyesCrop width comes from the distance between the eyes and a per-country ratio, and the eye line sits at a set fraction from the top. Minimum margins for hair, sides and shoulders keep the head from being clipped.
  • Background measured on its ownrembg with the u2net_human_seg model cuts the person out, so the wall behind them can be scored for brightness, saturation and variation against each country's thresholds. MediaPipe selfie segmentation is the fallback.
  • Text and watermarks without OCRPlain OpenCV finds stroke-like edges, joins them into blocks and filters the blocks by shape, which is enough to catch stamped text and watermarks.

What was hard

The segmentation model is the heaviest part of the service and runs on a small server. It loads on first use and unloads after 15 minutes idle, and no more than three analyses run at once.

Photos are personal, so they stay in server memory for the length of one check, are never written to disk, and are returned with no-store caching.

Results

The app is live with six country profiles. Each check comes back from the server as a named code, such as FACE_HEIGHT_BORDERLINE, and the page turns it into a plain-language note on what to fix. It gives technical guidance only, not legal or immigration advice.
